# Project-aware security check matrix

Use this matrix for a website or app only after inventorying its routes, dependencies, data flows, integrations and deployment model. Classify each signal as **Present**, **Absent in inspected source**, or **Needs external verification** and attach paths or account evidence. Absence is bounded to the inspected source and time. If the source and service configuration disagree, record the conflict and request review. Unknown data handling produces a question for the owner, never a false pass. [La Segundita's profile](PROJECT-SECURITY-PROFILE.md) is one application of these rules.

| Signal | Recommended security check | Compliance question (if applicable) | Automation potential | Human approval / evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every project: dependencies | Clean install, source-advisory audit, installed-path/version comparison, patch compatibility and dated risk review. | Does a contract or policy require a particular review cadence? | High for inventory and exact-delta detection; low for exploitability or exception decisions. | Owner approves bounded exceptions and upgrades after path/reachability review; retain audit and lockfile evidence. |
| Every project: secrets | Scan proposed commits and relevant history; keep credentials out of source and logs; scope and rotate exposed keys. | Are there breach-notification duties if a real key was exposed? | High for scan and change detection; uncertain findings need triage. | Administrator verifies live secret scope, revocation and rotation without publishing values. |
| Every project: configuration, headers and build | Typecheck/build, inspect environment separation, HTTPS, response headers, cache behavior and safe error output. | Are specific controls contractually required? | High for code/build/header probes; production configuration needs authorized access. | Owner checks deployed behavior and approves material config changes; do not equate a header declaration with a delivered header. |
| Every project: repo/hosting access | Review branch protection, CI token permissions, deploy rights, MFA, service accounts and recovery roles. | Are access logs or retention required by policy? | Partial inventory of workflow permissions and repo settings if read access exists. | Account administrator confirms actual grants, MFA and offboarding; least privilege is a human decision. |
| Login or accounts | Test authentication, authorization for each role/object, session lifecycle, cookie attributes, recovery and abuse controls. | Are account records personal data subject to notice, access or deletion rules? | Route/session inventory and repeatable negative tests. | Product/security owner defines roles, proves access boundaries and reviews provider settings. |
| Public forms, search or APIs | Validate untrusted input, output encoding, method/size limits, rate controls and CSRF where browser credentials or cookies make it applicable. | What user data is collected, why, and for how long? | Find routes/forms and test known invalid cases; context still matters. | Owner supplies data flow and retention facts; reviewer confirms threat model and abuse thresholds. |
| Uploads or user content | Enforce authorization, type/size/content checks, safe serving, moderation and deletion; inspect image/metadata exposure. | Are images or submitted content personal, licensed or subject to removal rights? | Inventory upload paths and verify configured limits. | Human reviews real content, moderation, consent and retention; a CMS-only upload is not automatically a public upload. |
| CMS or publishing | Review editor roles, MFA, dataset visibility, preview/publish rights, content validation, outbound links and audit logs. | Who may publish personal or third-party material? | Detect CMS packages, routes and schema fields; test local validation. | Service administrator confirms live roles/dataset settings; publisher validates representative authenticated workflow. |
| Webhooks | Verify signature/authentication, secret scope, replay/idempotency behavior, input and body limits, retries, logging and abuse controls. | Does webhook payload include personal or regulated data? | Static route inventory and valid/invalid-signature tests in a safe environment. | Provider/account owner confirms delivery settings, secret rotation and replay policy; signature use alone is not proof of replay resistance. |
| Background jobs or queues | Review trigger auth, retries, idempotency, concurrency, data retention and failure alerts. | Does scheduled processing change retention or transfer obligations? | Detect jobs/config and exercise repeatable failure cases. | Operator confirms production schedule, permissions and incident handling. |
| Analytics, cookies or third-party scripts | Inventory browser requests, scripts, identifiers, cross-border transfers, retention and configuration; review supply-chain and CSP fit. | Which privacy notices, consent, opt-out and data-processing rules may apply by jurisdiction? | Partial source inventory and browser/network measurements. | Owner and qualified privacy reviewer verify actual collection, vendor terms and jurisdiction; dependency presence alone does not prove collection or consent requirements. |
| Payments or checkout | Map payment data flow, tokenization, redirects/webhooks, fraud/refund controls and PCI DSS scope with the payment provider. | Who touches cardholder data, and what PCI DSS obligations follow from the actual flow? | Detect SDKs/routes and test webhook signatures; cannot assign final PCI scope automatically. | Payment/security specialist reviews provider evidence and scope. Outsourced checkout does not automatically mean compliance. |
| Personal, sensitive or regulated data | Inventory categories, data subjects, storage, sharing, access and retention; minimize and protect data. | Could GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, COPPA, HIPAA, sector rules or other laws apply given people, purpose and jurisdiction? | Partial schema/form inventory and data-flow prompts; no automatic legal classification. | Qualified legal/privacy reviewer and owner determine applicability and required controls from real practices. |
| Database or external storage | Review grants, encryption, backups, retention, deletion, access logs and exposed data endpoints. | Where is data stored/transferred, and what deletion or residency duties exist? | Detect clients/schema/config and test least-privilege paths where safe. | Administrator verifies actual settings, restore tests, data ownership and retention. |

## Decision boundary

1. Run the always-relevant rows for every project. Trigger conditional rows from confirmed code or externally verified business practice, not from brand or industry guesses.
2. Preserve an evidence link and timestamp for each classification. `Absent in inspected source` suppresses only the corresponding source-based test; request external verification for hosted features, connected accounts, offline operations and live data.
3. Separate a check's **applicability**, its **result**, and any **owner decision**. A scanner result cannot approve a known risk. Record exceptions with exact scope, reviewer and expiry; fail or reopen them on new findings, changed exposure or expiration.
4. Do not declare legal compliance, safe production status or a complete penetration test from repository discovery. Route potentially applicable law and sector obligations to qualified review.

## Reusable Skill Knowledge

- **Future Claude Code `SKILL.md`:** guide the agent through repository inventory, evidence-backed classification, conditional check selection, unknowns, and a human-review handoff. Never make this project's features universal defaults.
- **Reference files:** keep the generic matrix and example evidence vocabulary separate from each project's dated profile, risk register and CI baseline. Preserve existing project rules instead of copying or weakening them.
- **Future scripts:** a read-only inventory could enumerate routes, handlers, auth/CMS/payment/analytics packages, environment-variable names, workflows and candidate data fields, then draft a profile with path citations and `Needs external verification` flags. It must not print values, decide legal applicability, accept risk or edit CI automatically.
- **Codex-compatible workflow:** inspect first, classify with evidence, propose checks, validate claims against source, and stop at the approval boundary for credentials, account settings, fixes, production actions and risk acceptance. Phase 3D.2 could automate the read-only draft and evidence checks, while keeping human confirmation before any gate or configuration change.
